You've practiced your answers. You know the story of your best project and how you'd handle the "tell me about a conflict" question. What you can't rehearse in your head is whether your Wi-Fi holds up, whether your face is a dark smudge against a bright window, or whether the neighbor's leaf blower starts at 10:05.

The fix is to treat your setup like part of the interview prep. Think of it as a dress rehearsal for a small, temporary studio. The plan below is spread over 48 hours so you have time to fix what you find, not just discover it.

## Why 48 hours, not 48 minutes

A test run an hour before the call tells you what's broken. A test run two days before gives you time to do something about it: borrow a lamp, move your desk, buy a cheap headset, ask your landlord about the router, or book a quiet room at a library or coworking space.

You don't need expensive gear. You need a setup that is *predictable*. Interviewers forgive a plain wall and a laptop webcam. They are far less patient with repeated freezing and echo.

## The 48-hour timeline at a glance

| When | Focus | Main goal |
|---|---|---|
| 48 hours out | Audit | Confirm time, platform, and find your best spot |
| 36 hours out | Connection | Test internet at the interview's time of day; set up a backup |
| 24 hours out | Full dress rehearsal | Record yourself on the actual platform, in the actual clothes |
| 12 hours out | Lock it in | Charge, update, close, prepare the room |

| 10 minutes out | Join early | Open the link, test audio, breathe |

## 48 hours out: audit the basics

Start with logistics, because they cause more missed interviews than tech does.

- **Confirm the time zone.** If the employer is in another country or region, write the start time in both their time and yours. Daylight saving changes happen on different dates in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia, so the gap between you and the interviewer can shift for a few weeks a year. If the invite is ambiguous, ask: "Just to confirm, is 2:00 p.m. Eastern?"
- **Identify the platform.** Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet and others all behave a little differently. Open the invite link now and see what it asks for: a download, a browser extension, a login or camera permissions.
- **Update early.** Install any app updates today, not on interview morning, when a forced restart can eat twenty minutes.
- **Choose your spot.** Walk around your home and pick the room using three questions: Where is the best light? Where is the quietest corner? Where is the strongest Wi-Fi? You rarely get all three, so rank them. Quiet usually matters most, then light, then background.
- **Note the contact details.** Save the recruiter's phone number or email so you can reach them if something fails.

## 36 hours out: stress-test your connection

### Test at the same time of day

Your home internet at 2 p.m. on a Tuesday may behave very differently from 8 p.m. on a Friday when everyone else is streaming. Run a speed test around the time your interview is scheduled. Don't fixate on the headline download number; video calls rely on a *steady* connection and decent upload speed. A fast test result that swings wildly is worse than a modest one that stays flat.

Then do a longer test: join a free call with a friend (or a test meeting in your platform) for ten to fifteen minutes. Watch for freezing, delayed audio and your picture turning blocky.

### Improve what you can

- **Plug in if possible.** A cable from laptop to router is the single most reliable upgrade. A cheap adapter works if your laptop lacks a port.
- **Move closer to the router.** If a cable is impossible, sit in the same room, or at least with fewer walls in between.
- **Reduce the load.** Ask housemates to avoid large downloads, game updates and 4K streaming during your interview window. Pause cloud backups and automatic syncing on your own devices.
- **Close the extras.** Quit unused browser tabs and apps. Keep only the meeting app and your notes open.
- **Restart the router** the night before, not minutes before.

### Build your backup

Assume something may fail and decide in advance what you'll do:

1. **Phone hotspot.** Check that your mobile plan allows tethering and that your data allowance covers a video call. Practice connecting your laptop to it once, so you aren't hunting through settings under pressure.
2. **Phone as the fallback device.** Install the meeting app on your phone and stay logged in.
3. **Dial-in number.** Many invites include a phone number. Keep it handy as a last resort so you can at least continue the conversation by audio.
4. **A pre-written message.** Draft a short note you can send in seconds: "My connection dropped. I'm rejoining now through my backup. Apologies for the interruption."

Calmly executing a backup plan is not a mark against you. Most interviewers have been through the same thing.

## 24 hours out: the full dress rehearsal

This is the most valuable step. Open the real platform, join a test meeting alone (or with a friend) and record yourself answering two or three likely questions. Wear the clothes you plan to wear. Then watch the recording with the sound on.

### Check your camera framing

- Put the camera at eye level. Stack books under a laptop if needed. Looking down at a lens from below or above is unflattering and distracting.
- Frame yourself from mid-chest up with a little space above your head.
- Sit about an arm's length from the screen so your gestures stay visible without crowding.
- Look at the lens when you're making a key point, not just at the faces on screen. Place a small sticky note arrow near the camera as a reminder.

### Fix your lighting

Good lighting is mostly about direction, not brightness.

- **Face the light.** A window in front of you or slightly to one side is ideal. A window behind you turns you into a silhouette.
- **Avoid overhead-only light.** It creates shadows around your eyes. Add a desk lamp placed behind your screen, aimed toward you or bounced off a wall.
- **Keep color consistent.** Mixing warm bulbs and cool daylight can give you an odd skin tone. Use similar bulbs or rely on one dominant source.
- **Test at the right hour.** If your interview is in late afternoon, check the light at that time. Sunlight moves, and a spot that looks fine at noon may glare at 4 p.m.
- **Watch for glasses glare.** Tilt your screen slightly or move the lamp to the side if reflections appear.

### Check your sound

Audio quality shapes how competent and engaged you seem, often more than the picture does. When you watch your recording, ask whether you sound clear and close or distant and echoey.

- A wired headset or earbuds with a built-in mic usually beats a laptop mic. Wired avoids Bluetooth battery and pairing surprises.
- Soft furnishings reduce echo. Rugs, curtains, bookshelves and even a throw blanket hung behind the camera help in a bare room.
- Mute notifications on every device, including your phone and your watch.
- Say your name and a sentence aloud, then listen back for clipping, hum or keyboard clatter.

### Review your background

Keep it tidy and neutral. A plain wall, a bookshelf or a simple plant is fine. Remove anything distracting, personal or off-message, such as laundry, open cupboards or clutter. Virtual backgrounds can glitch around your hair and hands, so a blur effect or a real background is usually safer. If you're in a shared space, a folding screen or even a clothes rack with a sheet can create a clean backdrop.

## Managing a noisy home

You can't control everything, but you can plan around it.

- **Tell the people you live with** the exact time and ask for quiet. A sign on the door helps.
- **Sort out pets.** Feed and walk them beforehand, and close them in another room.
- **Know your noise calendar.** Garbage pickup, school runs, deliveries and construction often follow a pattern. If you can, request a different interview time.
- **Have an alternative location.** A reservable library room, a coworking day pass or a friend's spare room can be a better bet than a house you can't quiet. Test the Wi-Fi there too.
- **Plan a one-line apology** for interruptions: "Sorry about that, give me a moment." Then carry on. A calm recovery matters more than the noise itself.

## 12 hours out: lock it in

- Charge your laptop and keep it plugged in during the call. Charge your phone and backup headset too.
- Set the room up tonight: lamp positioned, chair adjusted, desk cleared.
- Put a glass of water within reach.
- Prepare a one-page notes sheet: the company's name, interviewers' names, your three strongest examples, and two questions to ask. Place it beside the screen, not on it.
- Turn off automatic updates and reminders if your system allows it.
- Do a short sleep-friendly wind-down. Fatigue is a bigger risk to your performance than a slightly imperfect lamp.

## One hour out: the final pass

1. Restart your computer, then open only what you need.
2. Run a quick mic and camera test in your platform's settings.
3. Check your lighting against the current sun.
4. Silence your phone but keep it nearby with the hotspot ready.
5. Tell the household it's time.
6. Stand up, stretch and take a few slow breaths.

## Ten minutes out: join early

Click the link five to ten minutes ahead. If there's a waiting room, you'll sit in it quietly, which is fine. Joining early gives you room to solve a surprise permission prompt or login issue without panic. Keep your camera on, smile when the interviewer joins and mention anything relevant right away, for example: "If my connection wobbles, I'll rejoin quickly on my phone."

## If something goes wrong during the call

- **Freezing or choppy video:** Turn off your camera briefly to save bandwidth, then tell the interviewer you're doing so.
- **Echo or feedback:** Mute when you're not speaking and switch to headphones.
- **Complete drop:** Rejoin through the same link. If that fails, switch to your hotspot or phone, then send your pre-written message.
- **Interruption at home:** Mute, deal with it, and resume without a long explanation.

## A reusable checklist

Save this for future interviews:

- [ ] Time zone and platform confirmed
- [ ] Software updated; link tested
- [ ] Connection tested at the interview's time of day
- [ ] Cable connected, or router distance minimized
- [ ] Hotspot and phone backup ready
- [ ] Camera at eye level, framed mid-chest
- [ ] Light in front, not behind
- [ ] Headset tested; recording reviewed
- [ ] Background tidy; notifications silenced
- [ ] Household informed; pets sorted
- [ ] Notes, water, charger in place

A good home studio won't get you the job. Your experience and answers will do that. But a calm, clear, well-lit setup removes distractions and lets those answers land. Spend an evening on it, and you can spend interview day thinking about the conversation instead of the connection.
