Moving into data analysis is mostly a question of sequencing: which tool you learn first, how long you give it, and how you prove it. Public sources are clearer than you might expect about what employers list in job postings, and quieter about whether any particular certificate gets you hired.

This guide sets out the skills that show up in employer postings for analyst work, a part-time learning plan with checkpoints, and a cost-and-duration comparison of self-study, certification and longer programmes. Entry requirements and typical pay vary by employer, state and country, so check current postings in your own market before you spend money.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Start with SQL. O*NET tags Oracle PL/SQL as a Hot Technology for Business Intelligence Analysts (O*NET OnLine, U.S. Department of Labor, 2026), and almost every analyst task begins with pulling the data.
> - Plan 6-18 months part-time to reach junior-analyst competence. For comparison, the Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate is designed for under 6 months at under 10 hours a week (Coursera/Google).
> - Certification is not a stated entry requirement. BLS says market research analyst roles typically need a bachelor's degree, with no prior work experience or on-the-job training required (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025); certificates are evidence, not a licence.

## The Skills Landscape

The clearest public picture of what analyst employers ask for comes from two federal sources.

O*NET, run by the U.S. Department of Labor, tracks what it calls Hot Technologies — the software requirements that appear most frequently across employer job postings for an occupation. For Business Intelligence Analysts it lists 100 of them (O*NET OnLine, 2026), plus 14 separately flagged In Demand skills drawn from posting text (O*NET OnLine, 2026). That tells you two useful things: the tool list is long, and no single product dominates it.

Within that list, O*NET singles out Microsoft Power BI as an In-Demand Hot Technology for Business Intelligence Analysts, alongside Alteryx, Apache Spark and Google Looker (O*NET OnLine, 2026). On the statistics side it flags SAS as In-Demand, with IBM SPSS Statistics and MATLAB also tagged as Hot Technologies (O*NET OnLine, 2026). Oracle PL/SQL is tagged as a Hot Technology for database work, and GitHub appears under application-server software (O*NET OnLine, 2026).

For the labour market itself, the closest occupation the Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks is market research analysts. BLS reports nearly a million of those jobs in its most recent estimate, projects employment to grow much faster than average over the decade, and attributes a large share of annual openings to replacement needs rather than new positions (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025).

On credentials, BLS says these roles typically require a bachelor's degree, with some employers requiring or preferring a master's (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). O*NET places Business Intelligence Analysts in Job Zone Four, meaning most workers hold a four-year degree though some do not (O*NET OnLine, 2026). Requirements differ by employer and by state, and more so outside the US.

## Core Skills to Learn

Learning times below are planning estimates for part-time study of roughly 8-10 hours a week, not guarantees.

- **SQL.** The querying language you use to get data out of a database before you can do anything else with it. O*NET tags Oracle PL/SQL as a Hot Technology for Business Intelligence Analysts (O*NET OnLine, 2026). Learn it inside the Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate on Coursera, a nine-course program covering in-demand skills aimed at entry-level analyst jobs, designed for under 6 months at under 10 hours a week and costing most learners under $300 at $49/month after a 7-day free trial (Coursera/Google). Estimate 6-10 weeks to working competence.
- **Spreadsheet data cleaning.** Most analyst work starts with messy exports. Spreadsheets are where you learn joins, lookups, pivot summaries and the habit of documenting what you changed. The same Google certificate covers this early (Coursera/Google). Estimate 3-5 weeks.
- **Power BI.** O*NET flags Microsoft Power BI as an In-Demand Hot Technology for Business Intelligence Analysts (O*NET OnLine, 2026). Study against Microsoft's published PL-300 skills outline, which devotes 25-30% of exam content to visualizing and analyzing data and 15-20% to deploying and maintaining assets (Microsoft); passing grants the Microsoft Certified: Power BI Data Analyst Associate credential (DataCamp). Estimate 8-12 weeks.
- **A second visualization tool.** Many teams run Tableau or Google Looker, which O*NET also lists as an In-Demand Hot Technology (O*NET OnLine, 2026). For Tableau, CIO.com describes a $1,000 exam-prep package with 90 days of eLearning access, biweekly live instructor sessions, 120+ practice questions and up to two exam vouchers (CIO.com). Estimate 4-8 weeks once you know one dashboard tool.
- **Statistics and a statistical tool.** Averages without variance mislead stakeholders. O*NET flags SAS as In-Demand, with IBM SPSS Statistics and MATLAB also tagged Hot Technologies (O*NET OnLine, 2026). The Google Advanced Data Analytics Professional Certificate is advertised for completion in under 6 months at under 10 hours a week (Coursera/Google). Estimate 3-5 months.
- **Python or R.** Used for repeatable cleaning, joins and modelling that spreadsheets cannot handle at volume; Apache Spark appears in O*NET's In-Demand list for larger-scale work (O*NET OnLine, 2026). The Advanced certificate above is one structured route (Coursera/Google). Estimate 3-6 months to script confidently.
- **Version control with GitHub.** O*NET tags GitHub as a Hot Technology for this occupation (O*NET OnLine, 2026). It also doubles as the place your portfolio lives. Estimate 1-2 weeks for the basics.
- **Written explanation of findings.** O*NET maintains a separate list of 14 In Demand skills drawn from posting text (O*NET OnLine, 2026), and these are the non-tool half of the job. Practise by writing a one-page summary for every project you finish. Ongoing.

## Building a Learning Plan

1. **Months 1-2: query and clean (8-10 hours a week).** Work through SQL and spreadsheet cleaning first, because every later skill depends on getting data in a usable shape. *Milestone:* you can answer five questions about a public dataset using only SQL, with no copying rows by hand.
2. **Months 2-4: build one dashboard tool to depth (8-10 hours a week).** Pick Power BI or Tableau based on what local postings ask for. Use Microsoft's PL-300 skills outline as a syllabus even if you do not sit the exam — it allocates 25-30% to visualizing and analyzing data and 15-20% to deploying and maintaining assets (Microsoft). *Milestone:* a published dashboard that someone who did not build it can read without you explaining it.
3. **Months 3-6: finish a structured certificate (under 10 hours a week).** The Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate is designed for under 6 months at that pace and costs most learners under $300 at $49/month (Coursera/Google). *Milestone:* certificate issued and all nine courses' project work saved to GitHub, which O*NET lists as a Hot Technology for this occupation (O*NET OnLine, 2026).
4. **Months 5-9: statistics and scripting (6-8 hours a week).** Add Python or R and the statistical reasoning behind your charts. The Google Advanced Data Analytics Professional Certificate is advertised for under 6 months at under 10 hours a week (Coursera/Google). *Milestone:* one project where you state an assumption, test it, and report the result including what it does not prove.
5. **Months 6-12: portfolio and applications (4-6 hours a week).** Three to five finished projects, each with a one-page written summary. *Milestone:* you can talk through any project for ten minutes without slides.
6. **Months 9-18: close the gap the postings name (2-4 hours a week).** Re-read 20 current postings in your state and list the tools you still lack — Alteryx, Apache Spark and Google Looker all appear in O*NET's In-Demand list (O*NET OnLine, 2026). *Milestone:* no tool appears in more than a quarter of your target postings without you having touched it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the cheapest credible way to start?+**

A subscription-based professional certificate. Coursera states most learners finish the Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate for under $300 in total, based on $49/month pricing after a 7-day free trial, and designs it for under 6 months at under 10 hours a week (Coursera/Google). Your cost falls if you finish faster, because you are paying by the month.

**Will a certificate get me hired without a degree?+**

The public data does not support that claim. BLS says market research analyst roles — the closest tracked occupation — typically require a bachelor's degree, with some employers requiring or preferring a master's (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). O*NET places Business Intelligence Analysts in Job Zone Four, where most workers hold a four-year degree though some do not (O*NET OnLine, 2026). A certificate plus a portfolio can make you a credible candidate; it does not change a stated degree requirement, and requirements differ by employer and state.

**How long until I am competent enough to apply?+**

Plan 6-18 months of part-time study. The lower end assumes you already use spreadsheets and SQL at work; the structured certificates on this page are each designed for under 6 months at under 10 hours a week (Coursera/Google), and most people need a second block of time for statistics, scripting and a portfolio on top.

**Power BI or Tableau first?+**

Check your own local postings, then pick one. O*NET flags Microsoft Power BI as an In-Demand Hot Technology for Business Intelligence Analysts, alongside Alteryx, Apache Spark and Google Looker (O*NET OnLine, 2026). On cost, Microsoft publishes a free PL-300 skills outline you can study against (Microsoft), while CIO.com describes a $1,000 Tableau exam-prep package including 90 days of eLearning and up to two exam vouchers (CIO.com).

**What happens if I fail a certification exam?+**

You pay to retake it. Simplilearn states Tableau retakes cost roughly $100 at Desktop Specialist level and about $250 at Desktop Certified Consultant level (Simplilearn). Some prep packages bundle vouchers — the $1,000 Tableau package described by CIO.com includes up to two (CIO.com). Confirm retake fees and waiting periods with the vendor before you book.

**Is the job market worth entering?+**

BLS reports nearly a million market research analyst jobs in its most recent estimate and projects employment to grow much faster than average over the decade, with a large share of annual openings coming from replacement needs (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). Pay is set by employer, sector and state, so check current BLS wage data and live postings for your own metro rather than national averages.
