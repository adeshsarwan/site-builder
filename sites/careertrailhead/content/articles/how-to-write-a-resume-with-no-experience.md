Writing a resume with no formal experience is mostly a sorting problem, not a writing problem. You almost certainly have material — classes, volunteering, a part-time job in an unrelated field, a project you finished on your own — and the task is to pick the parts that match the job you want, then describe them in the employer's words.

This guide takes you from gathering the raw material through to a saved, named file, with a template you can adapt at the end. Nothing here asks you to invent a job, a date or a duty you didn't do.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Mirror the posting: scan several ads for your target title and reuse the requirements that keep repeating (CareerOneStop, U.S. Dept. of Labor).
> - Avoid a skills-only functional layout — CareerOneStop warns an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is likely to reject it.
> - Quick win: name the file with your full name plus the job title before you attach it.

## What to Gather Before You Start

Open a blank document and paste all of this in before you write a single line of the resume. Drafting goes faster when you are editing material rather than hunting for it.

- **The job posting you are applying to**, copied as plain text so you can highlight the words it uses.
- **Five to ten other postings for the same target job title.** CareerOneStop (U.S. Department of Labor) recommends scanning a number of postings for your targeted title to find the requirements and keywords that come up most often.
- **The exact job title you are aiming for**, written at the top of your notes — "warehouse associate", "junior data analyst" — so every later choice is measured against it.
- **A list of the transferable skills named in those postings**, such as collaboration, people skills, precision and follow-through — the examples CareerOneStop gives — with a note beside each one you can actually evidence.
- **Every role you have held**, paid or unpaid: part-time shifts, internships, placements, casual work, a family business, a club committee. For each, write the organisation, the city, your title and the start and end dates by month and year.
- **Your education record**: school or college name, programme, graduation date (or expected date), plus a list of modules, labs or assignments that relate to the target job.
- **Three to five things you finished, each with a number attached.** CareerOneStop advises highlighting achievements with figures, offering phrasings like "Saved 125 hours per project", "Increased sales by 15%" or "Trained 32 new staff". Yours might be items processed per shift, people served, a group size you coordinated or a deadline you beat.
- **Certificates, licences and training**, with the issuing body and the date awarded.
- **A tools list**: software, equipment, languages, platforms you can use without supervision.
- **Your contact details**: phone, an email address that is just your name, your city and region, and a link to a portfolio or professional profile if you have one.

If an item has no number yet, leave it in anyway and estimate honestly later. If you cannot stand behind the figure, describe the work without one rather than inventing it.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Choose the layout before you type.** CareerOneStop describes three basic styles — chronological, functional and combination — and warns that a functional format, although it highlights transferable skills well, is likely to be rejected by an Applicant Tracking System (ATS). With little experience, a combination layout usually works best: a short skills-led top section, then dated education and experience underneath. Keep it to a single column of plain text, because tables, side columns, graphics and text placed in headers or footers are the elements parsers commonly mangle. Set your document up with one simple font and clear section headings, like this: `EDUCATION`. 2. **Write the header.** Name on the first line, then one line of contact details. For example: > **Template line — adapt, do not copy** > [Your Full Name] | [City, Region] | [phone] | [firstname.lastname@email.com] | [portfolio or profile URL] 3. **Write a short summary aimed at the employer.** CareerOneStop says a summary should state your top accomplishments or skills, and notes that experts differ on whether to include an objective — if you use one, make it specific and directed at the employer rather than general or self-serving. Two or three lines is plenty. Example: "Recent [qualification] graduate with [X] months of customer-facing retail experience and coursework in [subject], seeking the [Job Title] role at [Company Name]." 4. **Put education near the top while it is your strongest evidence.** Name the institution, the qualification, the date, then two or three relevant details. Example line: "Completed a [length] group project analysing [topic], presenting findings to a panel of [X] staff." 5. **Write the experience section, whatever the work was.** CareerOneStop advises that if you have little experience in the job you are applying for, you should emphasise the transferable skills you built in past jobs that relate to your new goal. Use the same structure in every bullet: action verb, the task, the measurable result. Example: "Served an average of [X] customers per shift, resolving [X] billing queries per week without escalation." 6. **Add project, volunteer and activity sections to fill the gap.** Format these exactly like jobs — title, organisation or project name, dates, bullets. Example: "Built a [tool or report] using [software], cutting the time to produce weekly figures by [X%] for a team of [X]." 7. **Consider the optional sections that fit you.** CareerOneStop lists professional memberships or associations, publications or other work products, and community involvement as sections to add depending on your circumstances. Add one only if it supports the target title. Example: "Member, [Association Name], since [Month Year]; attend monthly [topic] workshops." 8. **Build the skills section from your keyword notes.** Group the tools and skills that appeared most often across the postings you scanned, and only ones you would be comfortable demonstrating. Example: "Skills: [software], [software], inventory counting, scheduling, written reporting, [language] (conversational)." 9. **Edit for the first pass.** CareerOneStop notes that employers spend only a short time scanning a resume before deciding whether it meets the minimum qualifications, so the top third has to carry your strongest match. Cut anything that does not tie back to the target title, make verb tenses consistent, and read every bullet aloud to catch padding. 10. **Save and name the file.** CareerOneStop advises saving and sending as a PDF to preserve formatting unless another format is specified, and using a file name with your full name, or full name plus job title — the example given is Sarah-Smith-Marketing-Coordinator.docx. Keep a plain-text (ASCII) version too: CareerOneStop notes this may be required when a job site or applicant tracking system cannot read a Word-formatted resume.

## Example Snippet

Both excerpts below are templates to adapt, not copy. The brackets are deliberate — replace every one with your own detail, and delete any line you cannot evidence.

### Top section: header, summary, education

> Template to adapt, not copy
> 
> [Your Full Name] [City, Region] | [phone] | [firstname.lastname@email.com] | [profile or portfolio URL]
> 
> **SUMMARY** [Qualification] graduate with [X] months of [type of work] experience and coursework in [subject]. Comfortable with [software] and [software]. Applying for the [Job Title] role at [Company Name].
> 
> **EDUCATION** [Qualification], [Institution Name], [City] — [Month Year] (expected [Month Year]) - Completed a [length] project on [topic], presenting results to [X] classmates and [X] staff. - Relevant modules: [module], [module], [module].

### Experience and projects bullets

> Template to adapt, not copy
> 
> **EXPERIENCE** [Job Title], [Organisation Name], [City] — [Month Year] to [Month Year] - Processed [X] orders per shift and logged stock counts, reducing recount requests by [X%] over [X] months. - Trained [X] new seasonal staff on [system], shortening their first-week supervision time by [X] hours each.
> 
> **VOLUNTEER AND PROJECTS** [Project or Role Name], [Organisation Name] — [Month Year] to [Month Year] - Coordinated a team of [X] volunteers across [X] events, meeting every set-up deadline. - Rebuilt the [spreadsheet or record system] in [software], cutting weekly reporting time by [X%].

Notice the pattern in each bullet: an action verb, the task, then the result as a number. CareerOneStop suggests exactly this kind of quantified phrasing — "Saved 125 hours per project", "Increased sales by 15%", "Trained 32 new staff" — so use your own true figures in the same shape.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should my resume be if I have no experience?+**

One page is normally enough when your history is short — entry-level, student or career-start. CareerOneStop (U.S. Department of Labor) notes that two pages are acceptable for experienced professionals, and that cramming content onto one crowded page can get a resume passed over. So the page count follows the material, not a rule.

**Which file format should I send?+**

Unless the posting specifies otherwise, CareerOneStop advises saving and sending a PDF to preserve your formatting. Keep a plain-text (ASCII) version in reserve, which the same source notes may be required when a resume site or applicant tracking system cannot read a Word-formatted file. If the employer asks for .doc or .docx, send that instead.

**Should I include an objective statement?+**

CareerOneStop reports that career experts differ on this, and says that if you do use one it should be directed to the employer and specific rather than general or self-serving. A one-line statement naming the role and the company is fine; a sentence about what you hope to gain personally adds nothing an employer can act on.

**How far back should my history go, and does unrelated work count?+**

CareerOneStop's general rule is to include your previous years of work history. With a short record, include all of it — unrelated jobs included — and use the bullets to pull out the transferable skills that match the posting. A supermarket shift can evidence accuracy, scheduling and dealing with people.

**Should I put references on the resume?+**

Usually not. Keep the space for evidence and prepare a separate list of names, titles, organisations and contact details to supply when asked. Ask each person first, and tell them which role you applied for so they are not caught cold.

**Do I need to change it for every application?+**

Change the target job title, the summary and the skills order at minimum. That is the point of scanning several postings for your target title before you draft: the repeated requirements tell you which of your existing bullets to move up and which to cut for each employer.
