Changing fields means your job titles no longer explain what you can do, so the resume has to do that work instead. The examples below show two common pivots — a long-tenured worker moving to a new industry, and a shorter sideways step into an adjacent role — and how the layout changes between them.

Both are templates with bracketed placeholders. Fill in your real titles, dates and numbers; rewriting a responsibility for clarity is fine, inventing one is not.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Example 1 fits you if you have 8+ years in one field and are moving to a different industry: skills group first, job titles sit underneath.
> - Example 2 fits you if you are stepping into an adjacent role (such as admin to project coordinator): dates lead, with skill-style headings inside each job.
> - The formatting choice that differs most is where your employment dates sit — top of the page in Example 2, below a skills block in Example 1.

## Example Resumes & Cover Letters

Both resumes stay inside the same basic layout rules: 1–2 pages, left-aligned text, one-inch margins, a simple font such as Times New Roman, Cambria or Arial, at 10–12 point (CareerOneStop, undated). A person will typically scan your resume for 15–20 seconds, so your target-field evidence has to sit near the top (CareerOneStop, undated).

### Example 1: 10+ years in one industry, moving to a different industry

> TEMPLATE — replace every bracket before you send this
> 
> **[YOUR NAME]** [City, State] | [Phone] | [Email] | [Portfolio or professional profile URL]
> 
> **Summary** [X] years in [current field] moving into [target field]. Strongest in [target-field skill 1], [skill 2] and [skill 3], built by [specific responsibility you actually held]. Completed [certificate or course name, if you have one] in [year].
> 
> **Relevant Skills**
> 
> *[Skill heading taken from the job ad, e.g. Operations Coordination]* - Coordinated [type of work] across [X] teams, reducing [delay or error measure] by [X%] - Rebuilt [process name], cutting turnaround from [X] days to [Y] days
> 
> *[Second skill heading, e.g. Data & Reporting]* - Built [X] recurring reports in [software name], used by [X] managers each [week/month] - Tracked [budget or volume figure] and flagged [X] variances before period close
> 
> **Professional Experience** [Job Title], [Employer type or name], [City, State] — [Month Year]–[Month Year] [Job Title], [Employer type or name], [City, State] — [Month Year]–[Month Year]
> 
> **Education & Certifications** [Degree or credential], [Institution], [Year]

This is a combination format, which works well to highlight transferable skills, refocus experience for a career change, or minimize work gaps (CareerOneStop, undated). It puts the target-field evidence above job titles that would otherwise emphasize your lack of related experience — the main drawback of a straight chronological layout for career changers (CareerOneStop, undated). Crucially, the employer and date list is still there: a purely functional resume with no detailed work history can be misinterpreted by employers or rejected by an Applicant Tracking System (ATS), and is often viewed with suspicion because employers and dates are missing (CareerOneStop, undated).

### Example 2: Adjacent move, 3–7 years' experience (e.g. administrative role to project coordinator)

> TEMPLATE — replace every bracket before you send this
> 
> **[YOUR NAME]** [City, State] | [Phone] | [Email]
> 
> **Objective** [Current job title] seeking a [target job title] role, with [X] years supporting [type of projects] and [target-field skill].
> 
> **Professional Experience**
> 
> **[Current Job Title]**, [Employer], [City, State] — [Month Year]–Present *Project support:* Scheduled and tracked [X] concurrent projects, improving on-time completion by [X%] *Stakeholder communication:* Wrote status updates for [X] departments, reducing follow-up requests by [X%] *Process improvement:* Documented [process name], cutting onboarding time for new staff by [X] hours
> 
> **[Previous Job Title]**, [Employer], [City, State] — [Month Year]–[Month Year] *Administration:* Managed [calendar/records/vendor] workload for [X] staff with [X%] accuracy on [measure]
> 
> **Skills** [Software 1], [Software 2], [Method or tool], [Language if relevant]
> 
> **Education & Training** [Degree or credential], [Institution], [Year]

This is chronological with functional headings inside each job — the structure a worker used when moving from administrative assistant to project coordinator, because the move was a logical progression rather than a major career change, and the headings let her surface relevant project work (CareerOneStop, undated). Dates lead, which suits a steady work record (CareerOneStop, undated). The italic sub-labels do the sorting job that a separate skills block does in Example 1.

### Example 3: Cover letter opening for a career change (any level)

> TEMPLATE — replace every bracket before you send this
> 
> Dear [Hiring Manager Name or Hiring Team],
> 
> I am applying for the [job title] role at [organization]. I have spent [X] years in [current field], where [specific duty] was most of my week — the same [skill named in the job ad] your posting asks for. Last year I [action verb] [task], which [measurable result, e.g. raised X by X%].
> 
> I am making this move deliberately: I completed [course or certificate, if applicable] in [year] and have been doing [relevant volunteer, freelance or internal project work] since.

The letter is where you state the reason for the change in plain words, which the resume cannot do. Naming the skill from the posting and attaching one measured result to it keeps the opening concrete rather than aspirational. CareerOneStop's six-step career-change guide treats rebranding your resume as one step alongside researching the job market and exploring training (CareerOneStop, undated) — the letter is where that rebrand is explained.

## How to Customize These Examples

- **Rewrite the Summary line in Example 1** so "[target field]" uses the exact words in the job ad, not your own label for the work. Indeed's career-change guidance recommends adding a summary or objective, a skills section and relevant projects when you pivot (Indeed, undated).
- **Rename the skill headings** — "[Skill heading taken from the job ad]" in Example 1, and the italic sub-labels in Example 2 — to match the posting's terms. O*NET OnLine's Skills Search lets you build a list of your soft skills and see which occupations use them, which helps you name transferable skills accurately (O*NET OnLine, U.S. Department of Labor, undated).
- **Fill the Skills line in Example 2 with software you have actually used.** O*NET OnLine's Technology Skills Search finds occupations based on software used on the job, so you can check whether your tools carry into the target role (O*NET OnLine, U.S. Department of Labor, undated).
- **Set real dates in the Professional Experience block of both examples.** Use your actual months and years. If your history is long, include or emphasize only the jobs that add the most value, from the past 12–15 years (CareerOneStop, undated).
- **Replace every [X%] and [X] days** with a figure you can defend in an interview. An estimate you can explain is fine; a precise number you cannot source is not.
- **Delete the Certifications line from Example 1** if you have none, rather than padding it. Indeed's guidance lists certifications and courses as an optional showcase for career changers (Indeed, undated).
- **Strip any table, text box, logo, column, image or graphic** if you move these templates into a designed layout, and keep your name and contact details out of the page header or footer — an ATS may scramble that content (CareerOneStop, undated).
- **Swap the Example 3 greeting** for a named person when the posting gives one, and replace "[organization]" everywhere it appears.

## Frequently asked questions

**What file type should I send?+**

Send only the documents requested. If no format is specified, use Word, PDF or plain-text files, and avoid scanning a resume and sending it as an image, because it will not be recognized (CareerOneStop, undated). If the posting names a format, follow the posting.

**How do I shorten Example 1 to fit one page?+**

Resume length should be 1–2 pages, and you should only use two pages if your amount of experience makes it necessary (CareerOneStop, undated). To compress Example 1, cut the Relevant Skills block from three bullets per heading to two, and limit Professional Experience to jobs from the past 12–15 years (CareerOneStop, undated). Keep the dated employer list — removing it turns the resume functional, and a functional resume with no detailed work history can be misinterpreted by employers or rejected by an Applicant Tracking System (CareerOneStop, undated).

**Can I put the skills block in a side column to save space?+**

Avoid it. Tables, text boxes, logos, columns, images and graphics may be scrambled by an ATS (CareerOneStop, undated). Both examples are single-column and left-aligned with one-inch margins for that reason (CareerOneStop, undated). If you want the skills to stand out, use bold headings rather than a second column.

**Which example should I use if I have a gap between jobs?+**

Example 1. The combination format works well to highlight transferable skills from paid and unpaid work, refocus experience for a career change, or minimize work gaps (CareerOneStop, undated). Keep your real start and end dates in the Professional Experience block and let the skills section carry the first scan — a reader typically spends 15–20 seconds on it (CareerOneStop, undated).
