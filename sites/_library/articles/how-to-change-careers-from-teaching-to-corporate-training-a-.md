Moving from teaching into corporate training is one of the shorter career changes available to an educator, because the core work — designing instruction, delivering it, and measuring whether it stuck — is the same craft aimed at a different audience. The realistic planning window is 6-18 months from first research to first offer, longer if you are also finishing a school year.

This roadmap covers why teachers look at this move, the sequence of steps that gets you interview-ready, which of your skills transfer untouched and which you have to build, and a checklist to tell you whether to start now or in a year.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Plan for 6-18 months: roughly 2-3 months of research and vocabulary-building, 3-6 months of portfolio work, then a 3-9 month search.
> - The biggest obstacle is not skill — it is translation. Hiring managers screen out résumés written in school language (standards, IEPs, bell schedules) before a human reads them.
> - First action this week: open the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook entry for training and development specialists (2025) and the O*NET OnLine profile for the same role, and list every task you already do.

## Why People Make This Switch

Three market signals are worth checking yourself before you spend a year on this, and all three come from public, free sources.

**Projected growth, side by side.** The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes a 2025-35 employment projection for training and development specialists and a separate 2025-35 projection for high school teachers (both U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). Pull both numbers up in the same browser session and compare them directly rather than taking anyone's word for which is higher. BLS also publishes the projected average annual job openings for training and development specialists over the decade and the current total number of jobs nationwide (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025) — openings per year matters more to you than growth rate, because it counts replacement hiring as well as new positions.

**Pay structure, not just a median.** BLS reports a median annual wage for training and development specialists and a 10th-to-90th-percentile range for the occupation, and does the same for high school teachers (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). The shape of the two ranges differs: teacher pay in most districts runs on a published step-and-lane schedule tied to years served and credits earned, while corporate training pay is a negotiated band that can include a bonus and varies by employer, industry and state. BLS also publishes an industry-level median for training specialists working in educational services (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025), which is useful if you are considering staying in education but moving to a corporate-style learning role. Check the current figures on the BLS page and against your own state's wage data — none of these numbers are stable year to year.

**The credential barrier is lower than you expect.** BLS lists the typical entry-level education for training and development specialists (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025), and it does not require a teaching license or an education master's. If you already hold a bachelor's degree and a classroom record, the gap is usually experience and vocabulary, not schooling.

A longer-term path exists too: BLS publishes a separate median wage and 2025-35 projection for training and development managers (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025), which is the usual advancement step after several years as a specialist.

## Step-by-Step Transition Plan

Time estimates below are planning ranges for someone still teaching full time. Compress them if you are between contracts.

1. **Audit your tasks against the official role description (2-3 weeks).** Open the O*NET OnLine profile for Training and Development Specialists (U.S. Department of Labor). O*NET lists *Instructing* — teaching others how to do something — as a skill for the role, and *Education and Training* as a required knowledge area, defined as curriculum and training design, teaching and instruction for individuals and groups, and measurement of training effects (O*NET OnLine, undated). Write down a concrete example of each from your own teaching. O*NET also assigns the occupation a Job Zone classification showing how much preparation is typically expected — read it before you assume you need a new degree.
2. **Learn the vocabulary and pick a lane (4-8 weeks).** Corporate learning splits roughly into instructional design (you build the course), facilitation (you deliver it), and learning technology (you run the systems). Read live job postings and note which verbs repeat. Decide which lane you are targeting; a résumé aimed at all three usually reads as aimed at none.
3. **Join the Association for Talent Development and a local chapter (1-2 weeks to set up, then ongoing).** ATD is the main professional body for this field in the US. Local chapters — ATD-Los Angeles is one example of a chapter — run events where you meet practitioners who will later explain what a posting actually means. Membership and event fees vary; check ATD's current rates directly, as they change and differ by chapter.
4. **Build three portfolio artefacts (3-6 months, 4-6 hours a week).** Not lesson plans — a short e-learning module, a facilitator guide for a 60-minute workshop, and a one-page evaluation plan showing how you measured whether learning transferred. Build at least one for a non-school audience: a volunteer board, a small business, a nonprofit. Authoring-tool trials and free tiers are enough to start; licence costs vary widely by vendor, so price two or three before committing.
5. **Decide on an ATD certification — or decide against it (research 1-2 weeks, preparation 3-6 months if you proceed).** ATD offers the Associate Professional in Talent Development (APTD) for earlier-career practitioners and the Certified Professional in Talent Development (CPTD), formerly the CPLP, for more experienced ones; the CPTD exam content is pitched at a later career stage than the APTD (ATD Certification Institute, 2025). Each has its own eligibility requirements, and ATD publishes a pathway for APTD holders moving up to CPTD (ATD Certification Institute, 2024). The credential also has a fixed validity period with recertification requirements. Fees, eligibility rules and recertification cycles are set by the ATD Certification Institute and change — read the current handbook on their site rather than a summary.
6. **Rewrite your résumé in business language (2-3 weeks).** "Taught 150 students" becomes "designed and delivered a 180-hour curriculum to 150 learners across five cohorts, with assessment data at each checkpoint." Drop district-specific acronyms entirely.
7. **Run 8-12 informational conversations (2-3 months, overlapping with step 6).** Target people with the exact job title you want, not recruiters. Ask what their last three projects were and what a bad hire in their team got wrong.
8. **Apply in volume and track it (3-9 months).** Expect the search itself to be the longest phase. Adjacent entry points include training coordinator, onboarding specialist, sales enablement, customer education, and training roles inside educational-services companies.

## Skills & Qualifications: Old vs New

| Teaching asset | Corporate training equivalent | Transfers directly or must be learned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lesson and unit planning | Instructional design using a documented model such as ADDIE or backward design | Transfers directly; the method is the same, the artefacts and naming are not |
| Classroom delivery and managing a room | Facilitation of workshops, often to resistant adult learners with competing priorities | Transfers directly; expect to adjust for voluntary attendance and no grades |
| Assessment, rubrics and data-driven reteaching | Measuring training effects — O*NET names this within the Education and Training knowledge area for the role (O*NET OnLine, undated) | Transfers directly; you must relearn it in business terms such as behaviour change and performance metrics |
| State teaching licence and education coursework | Not required — BLS lists a different typical entry-level education for training and development specialists (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025); optional ATD APTD or CPTD credentials instead | Does not transfer; the licence carries little weight outside schools |
| Differentiating for student needs | Audience analysis and stakeholder interviews before a project starts | Partly transfers; the skill is there, the stakeholder-management layer is new |
| Gradebook, SIS and classroom tech | Learning management systems, authoring tools, and basic reporting | Must be learned; name the specific tools you can use on your résumé |
| School year planning | Project management on budgets, deadlines and a client sponsor who can change scope mid-build | Must be learned; this is the most common gap flagged in interviews |

## Readiness Checklist

Answer each of these yes or no. Six or more "no" answers means you are at the research stage, not the application stage.

- I have at least three months of essential living expenses saved in an accessible account.
- I have written down what happens to my health coverage and my state pension or retirement contributions if I leave mid-contract, and confirmed it with my district's HR office.
- I have read the current BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook pages for both training and development specialists and high school teachers (2025) and know the wage ranges and projections for each.
- I have checked whether my state or district requires notice by a specific date to resign without penalty.
- I can name the three lanes of corporate learning and say which one I am applying to.
- I have three portfolio artefacts I could send today, at least one built for a non-school audience.
- I can demonstrate at least one authoring tool and one learning management system without supervision.
- I have had at least five conversations with people who currently hold the job title I want.
- I have a résumé with zero district-specific acronyms on it, reviewed by someone who works outside education.
- I have decided whether I am pursuing an ATD credential and, if so, confirmed my eligibility and the current fee on the ATD Certification Institute site.
- I have a target date for my first application and a date at which I will reassess if nothing has landed.
- I know which adjacent job titles I will also apply to if training specialist roles do not convert.

**Will I earn more in corporate training than in teaching?+**

Not automatically, and anyone who promises you a number is guessing. BLS publishes a median annual wage and a 10th-to-90th-percentile range for training and development specialists, and the same pair for high school teachers (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025) — compare those two ranges for your own state, not just the national figures. BLS also publishes a separate median for training and development managers (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025) if you want to see the ceiling of the usual advancement path.

**How long does this actually take?+**

Six to eighteen months for most people doing it alongside a full teaching load. The portfolio phase is 3-6 months at four to six hours a week; the job search itself is typically 3-9 months. If you are targeting a first role inside an educational-services company rather than a corporate learning team, the search end can be shorter because your background reads as directly relevant. Add 3-6 months if you choose to sit an ATD certification exam first.

**What is the real risk that this does not work out?+**

Reduce that risk by setting a reassessment date before you start, and by applying to adjacent titles (training coordinator, onboarding specialist, customer education, sales enablement) alongside instructional design roles. **Do I need an ATD certification to get hired?+**

No. BLS lists the typical entry-level education for the role and it is not a credential in talent development (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). **Does my master's in education count for anything?+**
