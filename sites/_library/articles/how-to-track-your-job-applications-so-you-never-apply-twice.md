Applying twice to the same job is a small embarrassment with a real cost: it signals to a recruiter that you aren't paying attention, and it wastes an hour you could have spent on a new application. A single tracking sheet, updated the same day you apply, is the cheapest fix. Indeed notes that listing every job you apply for in a job search spreadsheet reduces the chance of applying for the same job twice (Indeed, undated).

This guide gives you the columns to build, the daily and weekly habits that keep the sheet honest, and three short follow-up emails you can adapt. Nothing here guarantees a response — what it does is make sure you always know what you sent, when, and to whom.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Build a Status column and update it after every step or piece of correspondence, so one glance tells you where each application stands (Indeed, undated).
> - Fill in an Application follow-up date when you apply: a few weeks after the deadline, or one to two weeks after submitting if no deadline was listed (Indeed, undated).
> - Add a Thank-you sent column and date it within 24 hours of each interview (Indeed, undated).

## Tracker Template Columns

Build this in whatever you already use — a spreadsheet app, a notes database, a printed grid. The tool matters far less than filling it in. Indeed's suggested column headings cover position title, company, job contact, date found, application deadline, date applied, application follow-up date, interview research, interview dates, thank-you notes, follow-up dates, status and notes (Indeed, undated).

| Column | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Position title (hyperlinked) | Linking the URL of the job posting to the title lets you relocate the ad when you're ready to apply or prepare (Indeed, undated). |
| Company | Sorting by company is the fastest way to spot that you've already applied to a role there. |
| Job contact | Recording the recruiter or hiring manager's name means your follow-up goes to a person, not a generic inbox. |
| Date found | Tells you how stale a posting is before you spend an hour on the application. |
| Application deadline | Lets you sequence your week around what closes first rather than what you saw most recently. |
| Date applied | The single field that proves you already applied, which is the whole point of the sheet. |
| Materials sent | After submitting, write down what you provided — cover letter, resume version, letters of recommendation — so you can repeat or reuse it (Indeed, undated). |
| Resume version used | Records which tailored file you sent, so an interview question about a specific metric or job-title match doesn't catch you out. |
| Application follow-up date | Choose a date a few weeks after the deadline, or one to two weeks after submitting if no deadline was listed (Indeed, undated). |
| Interview research | A place to park notes on the company and interviewers before each round, so prep isn't rebuilt from scratch. |
| Interview date | Anchors every other deadline in the row, including your thank-you note and post-interview follow-up. |
| Thank-you sent | A personalized thank-you email should go out within 24 hours of the interview, and marking the date keeps it on time (Indeed, undated). |
| Post-interview follow-up date | If the interviewer named a day you'd hear back, set this one or two days after it; if not, pick a date one to two weeks out (Indeed, undated). |
| Status | Update it after each step or piece of correspondence so you can see where you are in the process at a glance (Indeed, undated). |
| Notes | Use it for when you expect to hear back, questions about the role, and your impressions of the interview (Indeed, undated). |

## Daily/Weekly Tracking Habits

A tracker that gets updated "later" becomes a tracker you don't trust. Tie each action to a fixed point in your day or week.

- **Daily:** Log every application within one hour of submitting it — company, position title with the posting URL, date applied, and the materials you sent.
- **Daily:** Before you open a job board, sort your sheet by Company and scan the names of employers you're about to search, so you catch a repeat before you start writing.
- **Daily:** Submit two or three applications, which Indeed suggests as a daily volume to aim for (Indeed, undated), and tick each one off in the sheet as you go.
- **Daily:** Filter the Application follow-up date and Post-interview follow-up date columns for today's date, and send any email those rows call for.
- **Daily:** After any call, email or rejection, change the Status field for that row the same day (Indeed, undated).
- **Weekly:** Count the rows with a Date applied in the last seven days and compare it against the 10 to 15 weekly applications Indeed suggests aiming for (Indeed, undated).
- **Weekly:** Re-read the Notes column for every row still marked active and add anything you learned — a name, a start date, a hiring timeline.
- **Weekly:** Move every row with no response more than a month past its follow-up date into a "Closed – no response" status, so your active list stays short enough to work.

## Follow-Up Email Templates

Each of these is a skeleton, not a finished email. Replace every bracket, cut anything that isn't true of your situation, and read it aloud once before you send it. A follow-up is a prompt, not a guarantee of a reply.

### One week of silence after applying

> Personalize the bracketed details before sending — a generic version of this email reads like a mail merge.
> 
> Subject: Following up — [Position Title] application
> 
> Hi [Contact Name],
> 
> I applied for the [Position Title] role on [Date Applied] and wanted to make sure my application reached you. [One sentence naming something specific about the role or team and the relevant experience you'd bring.]
> 
> I'd be glad to send anything else that would help. Thanks for your time.
> 
> [Your Name]

Set the send date using the Application follow-up date column: a few weeks after the deadline, or one to two weeks after submitting if no deadline was listed (Indeed, undated).

### Post-interview thank-you

> Personalize this before sending — reference something that was actually said in the room.
> 
> Subject: Thank you — [Position Title] interview
> 
> Hi [Interviewer Name],
> 
> Thank you for talking with me on [Interview Date] about the [Position Title] role. [One sentence on a specific topic you discussed and why it made you more interested.]
> 
> I'm happy to answer anything that came up after we spoke.
> 
> Best, [Your Name]

Send this within 24 hours of the interview and mark the date in your Thank-you sent column (Indeed, undated).

### Status check at the three-week mark

> Personalize before sending — check your Notes column for the timeline you were actually given.
> 
> Subject: Checking in — [Position Title]
> 
> Hi [Interviewer or Contact Name],
> 
> I wanted to check in on the [Position Title] role following my interview on [Interview Date]. You mentioned you expected to make a decision around [Date Given], so I didn't want to miss an update.
> 
> I remain interested, and I'm happy to wait if the timeline has shifted.
> 
> Best, [Your Name]

If an interviewer gave you a day to expect to hear back, set this follow-up one or two days after it; with no date given, pick a date one to two weeks after the interview (Indeed, undated).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many applications should I be tracking in a given week?+**

Indeed suggests submitting two or three applications a day and aiming for 10 to 15 a week (Indeed, undated), so your Date applied column should show roughly that many fresh rows each week. If you're in your last semester of college, Indeed suggests spending 10 to 20 hours a week on the search (Indeed, undated). Treat these as targets to measure against, not a threshold that produces interviews.

**When should I mark an application dead?+**

Use the Status column rather than deleting the row — you still want the record so you don't apply again. A practical rule is to close out any application sitting a month past the date in its Application follow-up date column with no reply. Keep the Notes field intact, since a contact name there is useful if the company posts a similar role later.

**Isn't the 'applied' list on a job board enough?+**

LinkedIn's My Jobs page and Indeed's My Jobs section both keep a list of roles you applied to through that site, which helps, but neither captures applications you sent directly through a company's own careers portal or by email. Your spreadsheet is the only place that holds every channel, plus the Materials sent and Follow-up date columns those lists don't have.
