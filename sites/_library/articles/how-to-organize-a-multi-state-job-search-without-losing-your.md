Searching across several states is not one job search repeated. It is one search with extra variables stacked on top: different licensing bodies, different employer markets, time zones that shift your interview windows, and recruiters who will ask early whether you are already local. The thing that fails first is memory — which version of your resume went where, who you spoke to in which state, and what you promised about relocation.

This guide gives you a tracker structure, a fixed cadence for keeping it current, and three short follow-up emails you can adapt. Nothing here guarantees a reply; it keeps you from losing the replies you do earn.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Add a **State / Metro** column and a **Relocation Answer** column to your tracker — in a multi-state search, those two fields prevent the most embarrassing mistakes.
> - Set the **Follow-Up Date** field the moment you apply, not later: one week after applying, and again at the three-week mark if there is still silence.
> - Log every application within an hour of submitting, and run one weekly pass to close out anything with no reply after 30 days.

## Tracker Template Columns

Build this in a spreadsheet before you build it in an app. A sheet with these columns works on any device, exports cleanly, and does not lock your history behind a subscription. Add rows as you apply; sort by Follow-Up Date when you sit down to work.

| Column | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Date Applied | Anchors every follow-up date you set and tells you how stale a lead has gone. |
| Employer | Stops you from applying twice to the same company through two different job boards. |
| Role Title (as posted) | Recruiters search their system by the exact posted title, so quote it back to them in emails. |
| State / Metro | The core multi-state field — it drives time zone, licensing questions, and which address you put on the resume. |
| Source | Records where you found the posting (company careers page, a job board, a referral) so you can see which sources actually produce replies. |
| Resume Version Sent | Notes which keywords, job-title wording, and metrics you tailored for that posting, so an interviewer never surprises you with your own document. |
| Contact Name and Email | Gives you someone to address by name instead of sending a follow-up into a generic inbox. |
| Follow-Up Date | The single field that turns a list into a system; fill it in at the moment of applying, never afterwards. |
| Status | One word from a fixed set — Applied, Screening, Interviewing, Offer, Closed — so you can filter instead of reading. |
| Interview Date and Time Zone | Prevents the classic multi-state error of joining a call an hour late because the invite was in the employer's local time. |
| Relocation Answer | Records exactly what you told them about moving, timing, and who pays, so your story stays consistent across states. |
| License / Credential Needed | Flags roles where the state has its own requirement; verify current rules with that state's licensing board rather than assuming your home-state credential transfers. |
| Next Action | One short phrase — "send thank-you," "wait for panel" — so you can pick up the file cold a week later. |

If you need employer names to fill the Employer column in an unfamiliar state, CareerOneStop's Largest Employers tool lets a user create a list of the 150 largest employers in any state (CareerOneStop (U.S. Department of Labor), undated), and its Business Finder is used to find contact information about a specific business or list of businesses (CareerOneStop (U.S. Department of Labor), undated). For the postings themselves, its Job Finder tool lets users search for jobs in any location across the United States (CareerOneStop (U.S. Department of Labor), undated), which is useful when you are covering several metros at once.

## Daily/Weekly Tracking Habits

The tracker only works if updating it is part of applying, not a separate chore you do on Sunday night. Keep the daily list short enough that you never skip it.

- **Daily:** Log every application within one hour of submitting — employer, posted role title, state, source, and resume version. If you cannot log it, do not send it yet.
- **Daily:** Set the Follow-Up Date to seven days out at the same moment you log the application, before you close the tab.
- **Daily:** Open the tracker, sort by Follow-Up Date, and send every follow-up email that is due today, personalizing each one with the contact's name and the posted role title.
- **Daily:** Add the time zone to every interview invitation you accept and put the event in your calendar in your own local time.
- **Weekly:** Change the Status field on every row that moved this week, so nothing sits on "Applied" after a recruiter has already called.
- **Weekly:** Mark as Closed any application with no response 30 days after the Date Applied, and note in Next Action whether you would reapply to that employer.
- **Weekly:** Count rows added in the last seven days by state, and shift your search hours toward the metro producing replies rather than the one producing applications.
- **Weekly:** Rewrite the Relocation Answer for any state where your timeline changed, so the next recruiter hears the same dates as the last one.

## Follow-Up Email Templates

Three scenarios cover most of what a multi-state search needs. Each one below is a skeleton, not a finished email. Replace every bracketed field, delete anything that is not true of your situation, and read it aloud once before sending — a recruiter can tell when a message was pasted. Response rates vary widely by employer, role and market, so treat silence as normal rather than as a verdict.

### One week of silence after applying

Send on the Follow-Up Date you set when you logged the application.

> Subject: Application for [Role Title] — [Your Name]
> 
> Hi [Contact First Name],
> 
> I applied for the [Role Title] role in [City, State] on [Date Applied] and wanted to make sure my application reached you.
> 
> [One sentence on a specific requirement in the posting and the directly relevant thing you have done.]
> 
> I am planning my move to [State] and can interview on [time zone] mornings. Happy to send anything else that would help.
> 
> Thanks, [Your Name] — [Phone]

Personalize the middle sentence before sending; without it, this reads as a template and will be treated as one.

### Post-interview thank-you

Send within 24 hours of the call, then update Status and Next Action in the tracker.

> Subject: Thank you — [Role Title] interview on [Interview Date]
> 
> Hi [Interviewer First Name],
> 
> Thank you for the time on [Interview Date]. I appreciated hearing about [specific project, team problem, or detail they mentioned].
> 
> It confirmed my interest in the [Role Title] role, particularly [the part of the job you would do first and why you are ready for it].
> 
> If it is useful, I can send [work sample, reference, or start-date detail you discussed].
> 
> Best, [Your Name]

Personalize the detail you quote back. One accurate specific from the conversation does more than three lines of enthusiasm.

### Status check at three weeks

Use once. If there is still no reply, close the row at the 30-day mark.

> Subject: Checking in — [Role Title], [City, State]
> 
> Hi [Contact First Name],
> 
> Checking in on the [Role Title] position I applied for on [Date Applied]. I am still very interested and would like to know whether the role is still open.
> 
> My situation has not changed: I am available from [date] and able to relocate to [State] at my own arrangement.
> 
> If the search has closed, a quick note is all I need.
> 
> Thanks, [Your Name]

Personalize the availability line to match the Relocation Answer already recorded in your tracker, so your story stays identical across every state you are applying in.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many applications should I be tracking each week?+**

Track every application you send, without exception — a partially filled tracker is worse than none, because you stop trusting it. Set a weekly target you can realistically research and tailor for, then check the Source column each week to see which channels are actually producing replies and move your hours there.

**When should I mark an application dead?+**

Set Status to Closed 30 days after the Date Applied if you have sent the one-week follow-up and the three-week status check with no reply. Keep the row rather than deleting it, and use the Next Action field to note whether you would apply to that employer again.

**How do I keep states from blurring together?+**

The State / Metro, Relocation Answer and License / Credential Needed columns are what separate a multi-state tracker from an ordinary one — check all three before any call. Requirements and employer practices differ by state, so confirm credential rules with that state's licensing board rather than assuming, and use a practice run through CareerOneStop's Practice Job Application tool, which is meant to be used to get ready to complete real job applications (CareerOneStop (U.S. Department of Labor), undated), before filling in long employer forms.
