Moving from accounting into finance — FP&A, corporate development, equity research, investment analysis or financial planning — is one of the shorter career changes available to you, because the two fields share a language. You already read financial statements for a living. What changes is the direction of travel: accounting records what happened, finance makes a case about what happens next.

This guide sets out why people make the move, a sequenced plan with realistic time commitments, a skills comparison showing what transfers and what you build from scratch, and a readiness checklist to run before you hand anything in. Where figures matter — pay, growth, openings — it points you at the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and the certifying bodies rather than quoting numbers that go stale.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Plan on 12–24 months from first study session to first finance offer if you stay in a similar industry, and 24–36 months if you are also changing sector or chasing an analyst seat at a bank.
> - The biggest obstacle is not technical: it is that hiring managers read your resume as backward-looking. You need two or three forecasting or valuation artefacts with your name on them before interviews get serious.
> - First action this week: pull the financial analysts entry (13-2051.00) in the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook and on O*NET OnLine, and write down which three tasks listed there you have never done.

## Why People Make This Switch

Three signals are worth checking yourself before you commit, because they decide whether this move is worth 12–24 months of evenings.

**Pay positioning.** The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes a 2025 median annual pay figure for financial analysts and a separate 2025 median for accountants and auditors (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). Look both up in the Occupational Outlook Handbook and compare them directly — that difference, not a recruiter's pitch, is the honest size of the pay argument. Medians also hide enormous spread: finance pay is more often split into base plus bonus, and the bonus share rises as you move from FP&A toward investment roles, so two people with the same title can be far apart.

**Hiring volume and growth.** BLS projects employment change for financial analysts over 2025–2035 and publishes both the projected growth rate and the projected number of average annual openings over the decade (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). It publishes the equivalent projections for accountants and auditors (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). Read all four numbers together. Openings matter more than growth rate for a career changer, because most hiring in both fields comes from replacement need rather than new positions, and the two occupations are not the same size.

**The entry bar is one you already cleared.** BLS lists a bachelor's degree as the typical entry-level education for financial analysts, the same as for accountants and auditors (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). O*NET rates Financial and Investment Analysts as a Job Zone 4 occupation — considerable preparation needed — with a bachelor's degree as the education level most commonly needed (O*NET OnLine, U.S. Department of Labor). So the gate is experience and credentials, not a second degree. That is why this switch is shorter than most, and also why the competition for each seat includes people who already have finance experience.

None of this varies evenly. Demand concentrates in particular metros and sectors, licensing for client-facing advisory work differs by state and by country, and whether a given employer treats your audit background as an asset or a detour varies employer by employer. Check BLS state and metro data for your own location rather than the national figure.

## Step-by-Step Transition Plan

1. **Pick one destination, not "finance" (2–4 weeks).** Read the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook entry for financial analysts and the O*NET OnLine profile for Financial and Investment Analysts (13-2051.00) task by task (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025; O*NET OnLine, U.S. Department of Labor). Decide between corporate FP&A, corporate development, buy- or sell-side analysis, and personal financial planning. The credential, the hours and the hiring route differ for each, and choosing late costs you months.

1. **Close the modelling gap (3–6 months, 4–6 hours a week).** Build three-statement models from scratch, then a DCF and a comparable-company analysis. Use any structured financial modelling course your employer's learning budget will cover — compare at least two providers on hours of content and total price before paying, since both vary widely. The deliverable is not a certificate; it is three working models you can talk through, built on real filings.

1. **Start a credential that matches step 1 (9 months to 3+ years).** For investment and analysis roles, the CFA Program runs across three levels, Level I, II and III (CFA Institute). You can register for Level I via the CFA Institute's stated eligibility pathways, which include an education or work-experience route (CFA Institute) — read the current wording on their site, as it is updated. Level I is a computer-based, multiple-choice exam (CFA Institute), and Kaplan Schweser, a CFA Institute Prep Provider, publishes a recommended preparation time per level; budget against that published number, not your optimism (Kaplan Schweser). For client-facing financial planning, the CFP Board sets four core requirement categories — education, exam, experience and ethics (CFP Board) — with a multiple-choice certification exam (CFP Board) and experience satisfied through its stated hour-based options (CFP Board). Check both bodies' sites for current registration and exam fees before you commit; they change annually and vary by registration deadline.

1. **Check whether your existing credentials cut the work (1 week).** The CFP Board allows certain existing credentials to waive part of its education coursework requirement (CFP Board), and publishes an average completion time for that coursework (CFP Board). If you already hold a relevant designation or licence, confirm directly with the Board what it does and does not waive in your case — do not rely on forum summaries.

1. **Move sideways inside your current employer (3–9 months).** Volunteer for the budget cycle, the variance analysis, the business case for a capital purchase, or the integration workstream after an acquisition. This is the single highest-yield step: it converts study into something a hiring manager can verify by phone, and it costs you no income. Ask your manager in writing for one named forward-looking deliverable per quarter.

1. **Add the data skills the job postings ask for (2–4 months).** Pull twenty live postings for your target title and tally the tools named. Expect SQL, a BI tool, and increasingly Python for the more quantitative seats. Learn them against your own company's data, not tutorial data.

1. **Rebuild your resume around decisions, not duties (2–3 weeks).** Every line should name a forecast, a valuation, a recommendation, or a dollar decision you informed. Keep the accounting depth — it is a differentiator in FP&A and credit — but lead with the forward-looking work from steps 2 and 5.

1. **Run a 3–6 month search with a target list.** Thirty named employers, prioritised by your current industry, since industry knowledge is the part of your profile that does not transfer between sectors. Expect finance interviews to include a technical screen and, for some roles, a timed modelling test.

## Skills & Qualifications: Old vs New

| Area | Accounting (what you have) | Finance (what the role needs) | Transfers? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Financial statements | Preparing and auditing them under GAAP or IFRS | Reading them fast to find drivers and adjust for one-offs | Transfers directly — this is your largest head start |
| Time orientation | Historical accuracy, close cycles, reconciliation | Forecasting, scenario and sensitivity analysis | Partly — the mechanics transfer, the tolerance for estimates does not |
| Core credential | CPA, licensed through your state board of accountancy | CFA Program (three levels: I, II and III) for investment roles, or CFP certification for planning roles (CFA Institute; CFP Board) | Does not transfer — separate exams, though CFP Board waives part of its education coursework for some existing credentials (CFP Board) |
| Valuation | Impairment testing, fair value disclosure | DCF, comparables, precedent transactions, LBO basics | Learn from scratch for most accountants |
| Modelling | Schedules, workpapers, reconciliations in Excel | Dynamic three-statement models with switches and scenarios | Partly — Excel fluency transfers, model architecture does not |
| Controls and risk | Internal controls, audit testing, documentation | Risk framing for investment or capital allocation decisions | Transfers as judgement, reframed toward upside as well as downside |
| Communication | Memos, workpapers, audit findings | Recommendations with a position: buy, build, fund, decline | Learn from scratch — the hardest unlearning for most switchers |
| Entry education | Bachelor's degree typical at entry (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025) | Bachelor's degree typical at entry; O*NET Job Zone 4, considerable preparation needed (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025; O*NET OnLine) | Transfers — no second degree required |
| Data tooling | ERP, general ledger systems, Excel | SQL and BI tools; Python in quantitative seats | Partly — learn the query layer from scratch |

## Readiness Checklist

Answer each yes or no. Six or more "no" answers means you are 6–12 months from being ready to search, not from being ready to study.

- I have at least three months of essential expenses in cash, held separately from my current account.
- I have written down the specific target title and sector I am moving into, not "finance".
- I have looked up the 2025 BLS median pay figures for both financial analysts and accountants and auditors, plus the projected openings for each, and know what change I am asking for (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025).
- I have built at least two three-statement models from public filings without a template.
- I can walk through a DCF, including how I chose the discount rate and terminal value, out loud and unprompted.
- I have checked the current registration fee and published exam dates for either CFA Level I or the CFP certification exam on the certifying body's own site (CFA Institute; CFP Board).
- I have confirmed in writing with my manager that I can take on at least one forecasting, budgeting or business-case deliverable this quarter.
- I have had conversations with three people doing the exact target job, at three different employers.
- I have one person inside a target employer who would take a call about my application.
- I have pulled twenty current job postings for my target title and listed every tool and credential named in them.
- I have a dated plan with a decision point: if I have no finance interviews by a specific month, I change the target role or the sector.
- I have checked whether my target role requires any licence or registration in my state or country, and read the requirement on the regulator's own site rather than a summary.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will I take a pay cut?+**

Often yes in the short term, especially if you move from a senior accounting title into a mid-level analyst seat. The BLS publishes 2025 median annual pay for financial analysts and for accountants and auditors separately (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025) — compare the two medians for your state, not nationally, because the gap varies by metro. Also compare structure, not just totals: finance roles more often include a bonus tied to company or fund performance, which means a wider range of outcomes in both directions. Model a 12-month scenario where your bonus is zero before you accept.

**How long does the whole transition realistically take?+**

If you stay in your current industry and move into FP&A or corporate development, 12–24 months from first study session to offer is a reasonable plan. If you are also changing sector, or targeting equity research or an investment seat where the CFA Program is effectively expected, plan 24–36 months — the Program runs across three levels (CFA Institute), and Kaplan Schweser publishes a recommended preparation time per level that most candidates find is the floor rather than the ceiling (Kaplan Schweser). CFP certification is usually faster: the CFP Board publishes an average completion time for its education coursework, and sets exam, experience and ethics requirements alongside it (CFP Board).

**Do I need the CFA charter, or is my CPA enough?+**

It depends entirely on the destination. For FP&A, financial operations and corporate finance inside an operating company, a CPA plus demonstrated modelling work is frequently sufficient, and your technical accounting depth is a genuine differentiator. For investment analysis and research roles, the CFA Program is the common signal and your CPA will not substitute for it. For personal financial planning, CFP certification is the relevant credential, and the CFP Board notes that holding certain existing credentials can waive part of its education coursework requirement (CFP Board) — confirm your own case with the Board directly.

**What happens if it does not work?+**

The downside here is unusually mild compared with most career changes, because accountants and auditors have a large projected number of average annual openings over 2025–2035 (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025) and your licence does not lapse while you study. The realistic failure mode is not unemployment; it is spending 18 months and several thousand dollars on exams, not landing the move, and returning to accounting with better modelling skills. Set a dated decision point in advance so you choose to stop rather than drift.

**Should I do an MBA instead?+**

Compare it on entry requirements and cost. BLS lists a bachelor's degree as the typical entry-level education for financial analysts, and O*NET rates the occupation Job Zone 4 with a bachelor's as the education level most needed (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025; O*NET OnLine) — so a master's is not a gate for most analyst roles. An MBA is worth costing out if you are targeting investment banking associate entry or a structured rotational programme, where the recruiting channel runs through campuses. Price the full tuition plus forgone salary against the CFA or CFP route before deciding.

**Is it harder to switch after ten years in accounting?+**

Different, not uniformly harder. Ten years of audit or controllership makes you credible for FP&A manager, technical accounting advisory, credit analysis and transaction services, where sector knowledge is valued. It works against you for entry-level analyst programmes built around recent graduates. Target roles where the job description asks for deep statement knowledge and the word "partner" appears near "business" — and expect to answer directly why you are moving now.
