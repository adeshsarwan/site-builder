LinkedIn and Indeed both list jobs, but they reward different behaviour. Indeed is built around volume search across a very large pool of employers; LinkedIn is built around a profile that recruiters browse and message. If interviews are the goal, the fastest route is usually to use each one for the thing it is actually structured to do, rather than running the same generic applications through both.

This guide compares the free tiers, the paid tier that matters for job seekers, and the newer automated-application tools on each side — including which performance claims come from the platforms' own marketing rather than independent research.

> Key takeaways
> 
> - Use Indeed's saved search plus daily email alerts to catch newly posted hourly, local and high-volume roles before the applicant pool fills.
> - Use LinkedIn's free Jobs tab to find the hiring manager or a team member behind a posting, then apply on the employer's own site.
> - Consider LinkedIn Premium Career only if you will actually spend the 5 monthly InMail credits (LinkedIn, undated) on named people at target employers.

## Detailed Breakdown

**LinkedIn (free account)**

The free tier gives you the Jobs tab, job alerts, Easy Apply and the Open to Work setting. Its real advantage is not the job board — it is that every posting sits next to a company page, a list of employees and, often, the recruiter who posted it. That lets you do something Indeed cannot: find a human attached to the req.

Easy Apply is a plugin that lets you send an application without leaving LinkedIn, using basic profile information (Jobscan, undated). That speed cuts both ways. It is fine for a close-match role, but because it pulls your profile rather than a tailored document, you should make sure your profile headline and your three most recent job titles use the same wording as the roles you want, and that each role carries at least one measurable outcome. Otherwise you are submitting a generic file very quickly.

On Open to Work: LinkedIn says it takes steps to prevent LinkedIn Recruiter users at your current employer from seeing that you have shared career interests, but it cannot guarantee complete privacy (LinkedIn, undated). Treat the recruiters-only setting as a reduced risk, not a sealed one.

**LinkedIn Premium Career**

Pricing starts at US$39.99 per month per license, or US$239.88 per year per license — billed annually that is described as a 50% saving — and a Duo option lets two people each hold their own license under one shared subscription (LinkedIn, undated). Check the plans page for the price shown in your own country and currency before you commit.

What you get that matters for interviews: 5 InMail credits a month to message people outside your network, applicant insights on postings, and more than 25,000 LinkedIn Learning courses (LinkedIn, undated).

LinkedIn publishes several performance figures for Premium. It states that subscribers who apply using Top Choice Jobs are 2x more likely to get a message back from a recruiter (LinkedIn, June 2026), that Premium Career subscribers are 2.7x more likely to get hired on average (LinkedIn, January 2022), that InMail makes you 5.7x more likely to get a reply than cold email (LinkedIn, undated), and that Premium subscribers have 11x more connections on average (LinkedIn, July 2026). All four are platform-reported figures from LinkedIn's own data, not independent studies, and the connection figure in particular may reflect who buys Premium rather than what Premium does. Read them as marketing claims, not as expected results.

Five InMails a month is the constraint that should drive your decision. If you will write five specific, researched messages, the subscription has a job to do. If you will not, the free tier plus a polite connection request does most of the same work. When you do send one, personalise it — never send a template as-is:

> Sample InMail to a hiring manager — rewrite every bracketed part before sending
> 
> Hi [first name] — I saw the [exact job title] opening on your team. I spent the last [number] years doing [specific, comparable responsibility], most recently [one concrete result with a number]. Two quick questions: is [specific tool or process named in the posting] the main workflow for this role, and is the team hiring one person or several? Happy to send work samples either way.

**Indeed (free job seeker account)**

Indeed's case is coverage. It describes itself as the world's #1 job site citing Comscore Total Visits data (Indeed, March 2026), reports 3.5M+ employers hiring through it and 685M job seeker profiles worldwide (Indeed, undated), operates in over 60 countries as a subsidiary of Recruit Holdings (Indeed, undated), and states that 31 hires are made on Indeed every minute (Indeed, March 2026). Those last figures are company-reported and say nothing about your individual odds — but they do mean that for hourly, local, warehouse, healthcare support, admin and trades roles, Indeed will show you listings LinkedIn simply does not carry.

The limitation to plan around is paid placement. Employers can post up to 3 organic jobs a month free, while Sponsored Jobs get priority visibility at $0.10–$5.00+ per click with a $5/day minimum budget (Pin, undated). So the top of your results is shaped partly by employer spend and partly by relevance. Counter it by sorting by date rather than relevance, saving the search, and turning on daily email alerts so you see new postings on the day they appear. Any claim that applying within the first hour improves your callback odds is anecdotal — widely repeated by recruiters, not established by public research — but applying early costs you nothing.

**Indeed Apply For Me (limited test)**

Apply For Me drafts applications using your Indeed profile information, including experience, location, salary expectations and role preferences, and has been available to a limited number of US job seekers (Indeed Newsroom, 7 July 2026). Indeed states that, on average, applications submitted through it are 3.5x more likely to be a great fit for both candidate and employer than a typical application — again, a platform-reported figure from Indeed's own US data (Indeed Newsroom, 7 July 2026).

In August 2026, Indeed paused the automatic-submit mode and refocused on a version where you review every drafted application before it goes out, describing the whole thing as an early test rather than a full launch (Indeed Newsroom, 4 August 2026). Practically: do not build your search around it, do not assume you can access it, and if you can, read each draft before submitting — the draft is only as accurate as the profile behind it.

## Where to Access Each Tool

- **LinkedIn (free):** the Jobs tab in the top navigation bar of the signed-in site or mobile app — saved searches and alerts sit under 'Job alerts' on that same tab.
- **LinkedIn Premium Career:** the Premium plans and pricing page, reached from the 'Try Premium' prompt in the left rail of your LinkedIn home feed or the Premium item under the 'Me' menu; confirm the price and feature list shown for your country at checkout, since some features are not offered in every market.
- **Indeed (job seeker):** the Indeed job search page on your country's domain, then the 'My Jobs' page once signed in for saved searches, saved jobs and application status.
- **Indeed Apply For Me:** no standalone page — it surfaces as an in-product prompt on eligible listings for the limited US test group; for current availability, check the Indeed Newsroom announcement page rather than a product page.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is LinkedIn Premium Career worth paying for while I'm unemployed?+**

The honest test is whether you will use the 5 monthly InMail credits (LinkedIn, undated) on named recruiters and hiring managers at specific employers. If your plan is mostly browsing and applying, the free Jobs tab does that already and the subscription adds cost without changing your behaviour. If you do subscribe, note the annual billing is cheaper per month than monthly (LinkedIn, undated), but monthly gives you an easy exit after a short, focused push.

**Should I run both platforms, or commit to one?+**

Most people get more out of splitting them by task than by trying to rank them. Use Indeed's date-sorted saved searches and daily alerts to find openings across the widest set of employers, and use LinkedIn to identify who owns the role and what the team actually looks like before you apply. The exception is hourly, shift and trades work, where Indeed's coverage is deeper and LinkedIn may add little.

**Are Easy Apply and Apply For Me worth using, or should I apply on the company site?+**

One-click tools are a reasonable way to clear borderline matches quickly, but they send a generic version of you — Easy Apply draws on basic profile information (Jobscan, undated) and Apply For Me drafts from your Indeed profile (Indeed Newsroom, July 2026). For your top ten target roles, apply through the employer's own careers page with keywords and job titles matched to the posting. Reserve the fast tools for the rest of the list.

**How should I read the '2.7x more likely to get hired' type claims?+**

Every figure of that kind in this guide comes from the platform's own data — LinkedIn's hiring and reply multipliers (LinkedIn, 2022 and 2026) and Indeed's fit figure for Apply For Me (Indeed Newsroom, July 2026). None are independently audited, and none tell you how similar the compared groups were. Use them to understand what a feature is designed to do, not as a prediction of your own results.

**Does switching on Open to Work put my current job at risk?+**

LinkedIn says it takes steps to stop Recruiter users at your current employer from seeing your shared career interests, but states it cannot guarantee complete privacy (LinkedIn, undated). If discretion matters, use the recruiters-only setting rather than the public photo frame, and accept there is residual risk. An alternative is to leave it off and rely on direct outreach to named contacts instead.
